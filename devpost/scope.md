# Room to Speak

One photo of his own room becomes a talking board: tap the kettle, and the device says "Saya mau kopi" for him.

## The Unique Kernel
His own room becomes his vocabulary. The AI does the part a speech therapist normally does by hand: it finds the objects in one real photo that matter for daily needs and gives each one a first-person phrase. The result is a scene he recognizes, not a clip-art card.

## Who It's For
A family caring at home for a parent who had a stroke and now lives with aphasia. He understands everything said to him but can't find the words. The caregiver sets it up; he uses it.
Today they have the generic hospital card ("EAT / DRINK / TOILET / PAIN"), which he ignores because nothing on it looks like his life. Scene-based boards of his real surroundings would suit him better, but building one means a therapist outlining every object by hand, so families at home almost never get one.

## The Core Loop
- **Set up once (caregiver):** take one photo of the room he spends his day in. Soft circles appear on the things he'd want to talk about (kettle, radio, window, walker, the family photo), each with a short phrase. Keep, rename, or remove each one.
- **Every day (him):** open the full-screen photo, tap the object, and the device speaks the phrase. A fixed row of core words (yes, no, help, pain, toilet) is always there, even if the AI found nothing useful.
He comes back because it is the fastest way to be understood in his own room.

## Inspiration & Identity
Calm, quiet, and dignified. Speak mode is the photo itself: big soft rings, no text, no menus, no timeouts, instant voice. Set-up mode is plain and practical, for the caregiver. The accessibility rules are the look: large targets, high-contrast rings, and the core row always on screen.
Reference: visual scene displays, a known approach in AAC (tools that speak for people who can't). Research pointer for `3-prd`/`4-spec`: https://aac.unl.edu/ (AAC resources incl. visual scene display work).

## What "Working" Looks Like
Open the app and upload an ordinary, cluttered living-room photo. Soft circles appear one by one on the kettle, the radio, the window, the walker and a framed family photo, each with an Indonesian phrase. Remove one wrong spot and rename another. Switch to Speak mode, tap the kettle, and a calm voice says "Saya mau kopi." No menus, no typing, one touch. Close and reopen it, and the scene is still there.
The "oh, that's cool" beat is the tap on the kettle and the voice answering.
Demo language: Indonesian, with English captions in the video. *(Agent's recommendation from the idea notes; confirm at review.)*

## The POC Boundary
- One room photo → one AI call returns up to 8 objects with position + first-person phrase.
- Set-up review: keep / rename / remove each spot.
- Speak mode: full-screen photo, tappable spots, built-in device voice, always-on core row.
- Language chosen at set-up: Indonesian or English.
- One saved scene on the device (no accounts, no server database).

## Later
- More rooms / several scenes.
- A recorded family voice instead of the device voice.
- "Most used" phrases rising to the top.
- Installable tablet app for the bedside.

## Explicitly Cut
- **Medical or clinical claims.** It is a communication aid prototype, not therapy, and we can't back a clinical claim.
- **Speech recognition.** He can't produce the words; the product speaks *for* him, not listens to him.
- **Accounts, sync, sharing.** Not needed to prove the kernel on one device.
- **Showing a real patient on camera.** Only with explicit consent; the demo uses the builder's own room photo.
